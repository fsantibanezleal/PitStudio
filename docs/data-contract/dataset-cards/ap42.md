# EPA AP-42 §13.2.2 — unpaved-road emission factors

> The US EPA emission-factor method for dust from vehicles on unpaved roads, used as cited parameters for haul-road
> dust in case C3. · Part of: [Dataset cards](README.md) · Related: [C3 Dust](../../cases/c3-dust.md) ·
> [M16 Dust dispersion](../../methods/m16-dust-dispersion.md) · [Dust](../../theory/dust.md) ·

## What and why

Haul trucks on unpaved roads are a main dust source in an open pit. Case C3 needs an **emission rate** (mass of PM10
per vehicle-kilometre travelled) before any plume or particle model can spread it. The US EPA's *Compilation of Air
Emission Factors* (AP-42) is the standard public reference: chapter 13 covers miscellaneous sources including unpaved
roads (§13.2.2), and chapter 11 covers mineral products [1][2]. Section 11.9 (Western Surface Coal Mining) adds
equations for blasting, loading, dozers and haul trucks [3].

PitStudio uses AP-42 as **parameters with citation**: an equation form and constants, each tied to a page, not as a
dataset to copy.

## Datasheet

### Composition

- **Document:** AP-42, Fifth Edition, Volume I, chapter 13, §13.2.2 "Unpaved Roads"; final section dated November
  2006 [2].
- **What is extracted:** the emission-factor equation(s) for vehicles on unpaved roads, the constants per particle
  size class (including PM10), the precipitation correction and the input ranges for which the section says the
  equation applies, each with its page number.
- **Equation form** — verified on the primary PDF (November 2006 edition): equation 1a (industrial roads) with the
  constants of Table 13.2.2-2 and the source-condition ranges of Table 13.2.2-3 (p. 13.2.2-5), and the annual
  precipitation correction of equation 2 (P = days with at least 0.254 mm / 0.01 in of precipitation):

  $$E = k\left(\frac{s}{12}\right)^{a}\left(\frac{W}{3}\right)^{b}\cdot\frac{365 - P}{365}$$

  with $E$ the size-specific emission factor (mass per vehicle distance), $s$ the road-surface silt content (%),
  $W$ the mean vehicle weight (tons), $P$ the number of days per year with measurable precipitation, and $k$, $a$, $b$
  empirical constants per particle size class. **No constant is given here**; all are extracted from the PDF with
  page references.
- **Unit conversion** used once the units are confirmed: if $E$ is in lb per vehicle mile travelled (VMT), then
  $1\ \text{lb/VMT} = 0.45359237\ \text{kg} / 1.609344\ \text{km} \approx 0.2818\ \text{kg/VKT}$ (exact definitions
  of the pound and the mile), which gives the C3 KPI unit (PM10 kg/VKT).

## Assumptions and limits

- **Screening, not AERMOD.** The plume in C3 is a screening model. EPA's preferred steady-state plume model for
  regulatory use is AERMOD, current version 26135 [5]; PitStudio does not run it and makes no regulatory claim.
  PitStudio is educational software, not design or regulatory software.
- **Input ranges.** An emission factor is used only within the input ranges the section states; inputs outside them
  are flagged in the UI.
- **Quality rating.** Where the section rates a factor or equation, the rating is recorded with the extracted row.
- **Site inputs are assumptions.** Silt content and watering effectiveness at a real pit are not measured by
  PitStudio; they are scenario parameters with stated sources.
- **Verified values:** industrial-road constants k = 0.15 / 1.5 / 4.9 lb/VMT (PM2.5 / PM10 / PM30), a = 0.9 / 0.9 /
  0.7, b = 0.45; ranges silt 1.8–25.2 %, W 2–290 tons, speed 5–43 mph, 4–17 wheels, moisture 0.03–13 %. The
  watering control-efficiency curve is still UNVERIFIED and the UI flags it.
