# GNS granular surrogate (2-D)

> A graph network that learns one time step of granular flow from GPU DEM/MPM rollouts and replays dumps, stockpiles
> and bench collapses live in the browser. · Part of: [Models](README.md) · Related:

## What and why

A Warp DEM or Newton MPM run of a muck pile or a dump takes minutes to hours on the studio GPU and cannot run in a
browser at useful particle counts. A Graph Network-based Simulator (GNS) learns the per-step dynamics from such runs and
then rolls out new scenes itself. The original GNS generalised to longer rollouts and to an order of magnitude more
particles than seen in training [1]; for granular column collapse, a GNS surrogate was "hundreds of times faster" than
the high-fidelity simulator and handled more than twice the training particle count [2], and a 3-D variant reports a
300× speed-up [3]. PitStudio's beyond-SOTA claim is narrower and testable: **a 2-D GNS reproduces the repose angle and
run-out of held-out geometries within stated tolerances, live in the browser.**

## Model card

### Intended use

- Interactive 2-D granular scenes in the web (dump over a crest, stockpile growth, bench collapse) driven by the
  visitor, with the GPU-simulated rollout shown next to it for the same geometry.
- A surrogate for repeated, interpolative queries inside the training envelope of material and geometry.

### Out of scope

- 3-D flows, cohesive or wet material, particle-size segregation, and materials outside the training range.
- Replacing the physics engines: Warp DEM / Newton MPM remain the reference ([M7](../methods/m07-gpu-granular-physics.md)).
- Engineering design of dumps or stockpiles.

### Architecture

Encode–process–decode on a particle graph, written in **pure PyTorch** (no `torch_scatter`/`torch_cluster`) [1][4]:

- **Encoder.** Node features: the last $C = 5$ velocities, distances to walls clipped at the connectivity radius $R$
  (m), and a material embedding. Edges connect particles closer than $R$; edge features are the relative displacement
  $\mathbf p_i-\mathbf p_j$ (m) and its norm. MLPs embed both into a latent of width 128 (estimate).
- **Processor.** $M$ message-passing steps (about 10, estimate) with residual updates:

$$
\mathbf e'_{ij}=\phi_e\big(\mathbf e_{ij},\mathbf v_i,\mathbf v_j\big),\qquad
\mathbf v'_i=\phi_v\Big(\mathbf v_i,\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)}\mathbf e'_{ij}\Big)
$$

  $\mathbf v_i$ and $\mathbf e_{ij}$ are latent node and edge vectors (–), $\phi_e$, $\phi_v$ MLPs, $\mathcal N(i)$
  the neighbours of particle $i$ within $R$.
- **Decoder** to a per-particle acceleration $\ddot{\mathbf p}_i$ (m/s²), integrated with semi-implicit Euler [1]:

$$
\dot{\mathbf p}^{\,t+1}=\dot{\mathbf p}^{\,t}+\Delta t\,\ddot{\mathbf p}^{\,t},\qquad
\mathbf p^{\,t+1}=\mathbf p^{\,t}+\Delta t\,\dot{\mathbf p}^{\,t+1}
$$

  with $\Delta t$ the surrogate step (s). Training minimises the L2 error of normalised accelerations, with random-walk
  noise added to input velocities so rollouts stay stable [1].

About 1.5 M parameters (estimate). The sum over $\mathcal N(i)$ is written as `index_add_`, which exports to ONNX
`ScatterElements` with `reduction="add"` (available from opset 16; `max`/`min` from 18) [5]. **Neighbour search stays
outside the graph**: the browser builds a cell list in JS/WASM and passes `edge_index` as an input.

### Training data

Warp DEM (Hertz–Mindlin with rolling resistance) and Newton implicit-MPM rollouts of 2-D column collapses, dumps and
piles generated by the studio, with friction parameters from [DEM calibration](dem-calibration.md). All ours,
CC-BY-4.0. **Held-out geometries**: splits are grouped by geometry (slope height, width, base shape), never by time
step, so the test asks for generalisation to unseen layouts. The MIT-licensed `geoelements/gns` code and its Sand
datasets are cited as the reference implementation; it is not a dependency because its scatter/cluster extensions
lag torch 2.14 [4][6]. Card: [synthetic data](../data-contract/dataset-cards/synthetic-data.md).

### Budget (estimate)

**4–15 GPU-h, 2–6 GB VRAM** for about 0.3–1 M training steps on 1–2k-particle scenes. Measured by the runner probe
`studio bench` before any long run; the GPU probes are written but not yet run on the reference machine.

### Export and web lane

- **Opset 17**: the lowest in PitStudio's 17–19 range that carries `ScatterElements(reduction="add")`; opset 18 would
  only be needed for max/min aggregation. IR version **pinned to 10**; dynamo export with `verify=True`; onnxslim.
- Parity: ONNX Runtime CPU fp32 vs PyTorch fp32 on ≥ 200 golden steps (rtol 1e-3 / atol 1e-5 / max abs 1e-4), plus
  rollout-level parity (repose and run-out deltas) for fp16.
- Size at 4/2/1 bytes per parameter: about 6 / 3 / 1.5 MB → **~3 MB fp16, LIVE** (ORT-web WebGPU, WASM fallback). The
  live gate is ≤ 50 ms per step (estimate: 10–50 ms on WebGPU for ≤ 2k particles).
- **WebGPU risk.** ONNX Runtime 1.30 registers a WebGPU `ScatterElements` kernel [7], but whether it honours the
  `reduction` attribute is **UNVERIFIED**. Fallback for ≤ 2k particles: a **dense aggregation** that avoids
  `ScatterElements`. Its memory matters: a receiver-incidence matrix grows as $N\times E$ — at $N = 2{,}000$ particles
  and ~20 neighbours each ($E \approx 40{,}000$) that is $8\times10^7$ entries, 320 MB in fp32 — whereas a padded
  neighbour layout ($N \times K \times 128$ with $K = 32$) is about 33 MB. The spec fixes the layout.
- **TensorRT relevance — low and risky.** TensorRT issue **#3650** reports `ScatterElements` with reduction failing when
  there are more indices than outputs — exactly the GNS case, where edges outnumber nodes [8]. The bench tests 1k, 2k
  and 10k particles; if the engine fails parity it is reported as rejected and the dense path is tried. Expected gain
  1–2× over eager (estimate): the model is gather/scatter-bound with a dynamic edge count.

### Pre-registered acceptance criteria

- **Repose ±1.5°, run-out ±5 % on held-out geometries**: $|\hat\theta_r-\theta_r|\le 1.5^\circ$ and
  $|\hat L-L|/L\le 0.05$, where $\theta_r$ (°) is the repose angle and $L$ (m) the run-out of the reference DEM/MPM
  rollout for the same geometry.
- Any "better than" statement (e.g. against a coarser baseline) only by the decision rule
  ([DEC-0016](../architecture/decisions/DEC-0016-pre-registered-decision-rule.md)).

### Evaluation protocol

- Full rollouts from the initial state of each held-out geometry, compared with the reference at matched times.
- $\theta_r$ = angle of the least-squares line through the final free surface between toe and crest; $L$ = horizontal
  distance from the original toe to the front at rest (exact percentile of the front fixed in the surrogates spec).
- Also reported: per-step position error growth, mass conservation (particle count is exact by construction), and
  speed (steps/s) in Python and in the browser.

## Assumptions and limits

- 2-D only; one material family per model; validity limited to the trained ranges of particle count, friction and
  geometry size. Outside them a rollout is an extrapolation and carries no accuracy claim.
- The reference is itself a calibrated simulation, not a field measurement: the claim is surrogate fidelity, not
  physical truth.
- Long rollouts drift; the tolerance is checked on the final pile, where drift accumulates most.
- Simulation-grade twin, not a live digital twin.
