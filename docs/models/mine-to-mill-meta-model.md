# Mine-to-mill meta-model

> A small gradient-boosted or MLP regressor that reproduces the analytical blast-to-mill chain (fragmentation → crushing
> → grinding) in well under a millisecond, so energy and throughput trade-offs can be swept live. · Part of:
> [Models](README.md) · Related: [M17 Comminution and mine-to-mill](../methods/m17-comminution-mine-to-mill.md) ·
> [Case D2](../cases/d2-mine-to-mill.md) · [Comminution theory](../theory/comminution.md) ·
> [minephys](../frameworks/minephys.md)

## What and why

Finer blasting costs more explosive but can raise mill throughput and cut grinding energy; mine-to-mill studies trace
that trade-off from the muck-pile size distribution to the plant [1]. In PitStudio the chain is analytical and lives in
`minephys.comminution`: Swebrec fragment distributions from the blast models [6], then Bond / Morrell energy–size
relations and population-balance (PBM) circuit models [2][3][4]. Evaluating the full chain for every slider position of a
multi-parameter sweep is the slow part, so case D2 fits a **meta-model** — a learned surrogate of the chain — on
`minephys` sweeps. The analytical chain stays the reference; the meta-model is accepted only if it reproduces it.

## Model card

### Intended use

- Instant estimates of kWh/t and t/h across blast and circuit parameters (e.g. $x_{50}$ from D1, ore hardness, circuit
  power) for D2's interactive response surfaces.
- A transparent example of a learned surrogate validated against a known function.

### Out of scope

- Plant design or any site-specific prediction: inputs are cited generic parameters, not survey data.
- Extrapolation outside the sweep ranges; real plant data (none open, so none claimed).

### Architecture

Two candidate families named by the plan, both small:

- **Gradient-boosted trees (GBM)** — scikit-learn 1.9.1 (BSD-3, in the `pipeline/` lock) histogram gradient boosting.
  Each stage adds a tree fitted to the negative gradient of the loss [5]:
  $F_m(\mathbf x)=F_{m-1}(\mathbf x)+\nu\,h_m(\mathbf x)$, with shrinkage $\nu\in(0,1]$ and tree $h_m$.
- **MLP** — a few fully connected layers in PyTorch.

The rule for choosing between them is fixed in the planning-survey feature spec; both are judged by the same criterion.

**The function being learned.** The anchor is Bond's third law [2]:

$$
W=10\,W_i\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{80}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{F_{80}}}\right)
$$

$W$ specific energy (kWh/t), $W_i$ the Bond work index (kWh/t), $F_{80}$ and $P_{80}$ the 80 %-passing sizes of feed
and product (µm). Mill throughput follows from power: $\dot m = P_{\text{mill}}/W$ (t/h for $P_{\text{mill}}$ in kW).
Morrell's alternative relation and the PBM add circuit detail; their constants are flagged UNVERIFIED until pinned at
specification [3][4].

**Worked example (illustrative inputs, not cited values).** With $W_i = 14$ kWh/t, $F_{80} = 10{,}000$ µm and
$P_{80} = 150$ µm: $W = 10\cdot14\cdot(0.08165-0.01000) = 10.0$ kWh/t; a 15 MW circuit then processes about
15,000 / 10.0 ≈ 1,500 t/h. Halving $F_{80}$ through finer blasting lowers $W$ only to about 9.5 kWh/t — which is why the
interesting effects come from the full chain (fines, SAG behaviour), not from Bond's law alone.

### Training data

Sweeps of the `minephys` chain (Apache-2.0 code; parameters from cited tables, each with its verification status):
blast inputs → Swebrec PSD → crusher → grinding circuit → kWh/t and t/h. Generated by the pipeline, CC-BY-4.0.
**Held-out sweeps**: whole sweeps (e.g. an ore-hardness or circuit configuration) are held out, not random points, since
points inside one sweep are strongly correlated.

### Budget (estimate)

**Minutes** of CPU or GPU time; no long run. The runner records it like any other stage; the GPU capability probes are
written but not yet run on the reference machine.

### Export and web lane

- **Opset 17**, IR version **pinned to 10**. The MLP exports with `torch.onnx.export(dynamo=True, verify=True)`; a GBM
  needs a tree converter to the `ai.onnx.ml` tree-ensemble operator, which is not yet in the lock (UNVERIFIED —
  pinned at specification), and runs on the WASM execution provider in the browser.
- Parity: ONNX Runtime CPU fp32 vs the Python model on ≥ 200 golden inputs (rtol 1e-3 / atol 1e-5 / max abs 1e-4).
- **< 1 MB → LIVE** (ORT-web). The analytical chain itself is also live (TS port and `minephys` via Pyodide), as the
  reference.
- **TensorRT relevance — none**: sub-millisecond on CPU (estimate); not a meaningful acceleration target.

### Pre-registered acceptance criteria

- **$R^2 \ge 0.95$ vs the analytical chain on held-out sweeps**, per output (kWh/t, t/h):

$$
R^2=1-\frac{\sum_i (y_i-\hat y_i)^2}{\sum_i (y_i-\bar y)^2}
$$

  with $y_i$ the chain output and $\hat y_i$ the meta-model output on held-out point $i$.
- Any "better than" statement only by the decision rule
  ([DEC-0016](../architecture/decisions/DEC-0016-pre-registered-decision-rule.md)).

### Evaluation protocol

- $R^2$, maximum absolute error and error maps over each held-out sweep.
- Monotonicity checks that must hold for both chain and meta-model (e.g. lower $P_{80}$ target → higher $W$; higher
  $W_i$ → higher $W$), reported as pass/fail.
- Timing of chain vs meta-model in Python and in the browser.

### Licence of weights

Own models; scikit-learn (BSD-3) and PyTorch are tools, not inputs. Training data are our CC-BY-4.0 sweeps of
Apache-2.0 code with cited parameters → weights **Apache-2.0 + attribution** (the parameter sources are listed in the
card).

## Assumptions and limits

- The meta-model can be no more correct than the chain it imitates; the chain's own limits (generic parameters, steady
  state, UNVERIFIED constants) carry over.
- Valid only inside the sweep box; outside it the result is extrapolation.
- Educational; simulation-grade twin, not a live digital twin of any plant.
