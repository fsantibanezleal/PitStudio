## What and why

Each time an empty truck leaves a dump, a dispatcher decides which shovel it goes to. Mines use heuristics (nearest,
shortest queue, SPTF — shortest processing time first) or a two-stage LP that sets target flows per shovel→dump path
and then assigns trucks in real time [1][2]. Reinforcement-learning dispatchers report gains — 5.56 % productivity over
an industry baseline [3], about 10 % over plain PPO on the OpenMines simulator [4] — but each on its own simulator and
baselines. PitStudio trains two policies, **M4 (PPO, MLP)** and **M5 (attention, size-invariant)**, in one environment
and accepts a gain only if paired seeds say so.

## Model card

### Intended use

- Real-time choice of the next shovel (and dump) for each empty truck in the A1 pit, fleets of up to 40 trucks.
- A live in-browser comparison against fixed, nearest, SPTF, shortest-queue and LP dispatch on the same seeds.

### Out of scope

- Any real fleet-management system; there is no telemetry feed and the cycle data are calibrated synthetic.
- Blending, plant-feed or grade constraints beyond those in the LP; breakdown and shift-change policies.

### Architecture

**Environment.** A batched PyTorch tensor environment: $B$ parallel copies of the mine (e.g. 4,096) × up to 40 trucks,
event-stepped with a fixed decision tick and a **masked** action space ("which shovel / dump next"); it runs on CUDA and
needs no Isaac software. Cycle components (load, haul by grade, dump, return) come from the same calibrated samplers as
the DES, so the policy can be cross-checked in the independent `minehaulsim` DES (Apache-2.0, 0.12.1 in the
`pipeline/` lock) [5].

**M4 — PPO.** A parameter-shared MLP over per-truck features (position, load state, queue lengths, travel times).
PPO maximises the clipped surrogate [6]:

$$
L^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta)=\hat{\mathbb E}_t\Big[\min\big(r_t(\theta)\hat A_t,\ \operatorname{clip}(r_t(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\,\hat A_t\big)\Big],
\qquad r_t(\theta)=\frac{\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t\mid s_t)}
$$

with $\epsilon$ the clip range (–), minus a value loss and plus an entropy bonus. Advantages use generalised advantage
estimation [7]:

$$
\hat A_t=\sum_{l\ge 0}(\gamma\lambda)^l\,\delta_{t+l},\qquad \delta_t=r_t+\gamma V(s_{t+1})-V(s_t)
$$

$\gamma$ is the discount and $\lambda$ the GAE parameter (both –), $r_t$ the reward (here tonnes delivered, with the
exact shaping fixed in the haulage spec). Invalid actions get logit $-\infty$ before the softmax.

**M5 — attention.** Each truck's embedding queries the set of shovels and dumps with scaled dot-product attention [8]:

$$
\operatorname{Attn}(Q,K,V)=\operatorname{softmax}\!\Big(\frac{QK^{\mathsf T}}{\sqrt{d_k}}+M\Big)V
$$

$Q$ holds truck queries, $K, V$ shovel/dump keys and values, $d_k$ the key width and $M$ the mask (0 or $-\infty$).
The same weights serve 10–40 trucks, so the policy is size-invariant — something the fixed-vector DQN/PPO formulations
of the published work are not. Both policies have about 0.1–0.5 M parameters (estimate). Mining-specific graph or
attention dispatch has no published precedent among the sources read, so M5 is a frontier claim to be validated, not
assumed.

### Training data

No dataset: experience is generated by the vectorised environment on the A1 road network (USGS 3DEP Bingham, public
domain) with calibrated synthetic cycle samplers (ours, CC-BY-4.0). Training, validation and test seeds are disjoint.

### Budget (estimate)

**1–5 GPU-h, 2–4 GB VRAM**: about 0.2–1 M decisions/s, so 100–300 M decisions in roughly 1–4 h per policy (estimate).
Measured by the runner probe `studio bench` before any long run; the GPU probes are written but not yet run on the
reference machine.

### Export and web lane

- **Opset 17**: Gemm/MatMul/Softmax only; nothing needs a later opset. IR version **pinned to 10**; dynamo export with
  `verify=True`; inputs padded to 40 trucks with a mask; argmax taken in JavaScript.
- Parity: ONNX Runtime CPU fp32 vs PyTorch fp32 on ≥ 200 golden states (rtol 1e-3 / atol 1e-5 / max abs 1e-4, 100 %
  argmax agreement).
- Size **< 2 MB → LIVE** (ORT-web WASM or WebGPU) against the TS twin of the DES; one shift (≈ 10⁴ events) runs in
  < 1 s (estimate).
- **TensorRT relevance — expected "no material gain".** A 0.1–0.5 M-parameter network at batch 1 is launch-bound; ONNX
  Runtime on CPU may win. Gains of 2–5× are plausible only at batch 4,096, as in studio-side paired-seed evaluation
  (estimates). That negative result is published as such, with per-engine parity
  ([DEC-0010](../architecture/decisions/DEC-0010-tensorrt-per-engine-parity.md)).

### Pre-registered acceptance criteria

- **≥ 30 paired seeds.**
- **"Beats SPTF / LP" only by the decision rule on t/h**: for seeds $s=1\dots n$, $\Delta_s=X_{\text{policy}}(s)-X_{\text{baseline}}(s)$
  (t/h, same mine, same load-time and breakdown draws); the claim needs the paired 95 % CI of $\bar\Delta$ to exclude 0,
  otherwise "no significant difference" ([DEC-0016](../architecture/decisions/DEC-0016-pre-registered-decision-rule.md)).

Worked example (illustrative numbers, not results): with $n = 30$, $\bar\Delta = 40$ t/h and a standard deviation of
the differences $s_\Delta = 90$ t/h, a paired-$t$ interval is $40 \pm 2.045\cdot 90/\sqrt{30} = 40 \pm 33.6$ t/h, i.e.
[6.4, 73.6] — it excludes 0, so "better" could be shown. With $s_\Delta = 120$ t/h the half-width is 44.8 t/h and the
UI would say "no significant difference". The interval construction itself is fixed in DEC-0016.

### Evaluation protocol

- Baselines on identical seeds: fixed allocation, nearest, SPTF and shortest queue (the `minehaulsim` dispatchers) and
  the two-stage LP (HiGHS).
- **DES cross-check:** each policy is also run inside `minehaulsim`; the gap between the training environment and the
  DES is reported, so the policy is not judged only in the simulator it was trained in.
- KPIs per dispatcher: t/h, queue time, shovel utilisation, match factor and cost/t, each with paired CIs.

### Licence of weights

Own environment, policy code and training (Apache-2.0); `minehaulsim` is Apache-2.0; terrain is public domain →
weights **Apache-2.0 + attribution**.

## Assumptions and limits

- A policy that wins in the vectorised environment may not win in the DES; only the DES-checked, paired-seed numbers
  count.
- Cycle times are calibrated synthetic, not fleet data; the claim is about this model of this pit.
- Published RL gains [3][4] do not transfer and are not used as priors.
- Simulation-grade twin, not a live digital twin.
