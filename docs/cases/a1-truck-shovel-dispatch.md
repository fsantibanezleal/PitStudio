# A1 — Truck–shovel cycle, dispatch and fleet sizing

> How many trucks per shovel, and which dispatch rule, maximise tonnes per hour at the lowest cost per tonne on a real
> pit road network — and does a learned dispatcher beat the classical rules? · Part of: [Cases](README.md) ·
> Related: [Haulage theory](../theory/haulage.md) · [M2 Haulage DES](../methods/m02-haulage-des.md) ·
> [Dispatch policies](../models/dispatch-policies.md) · [A2](a2-haul-road-electrification.md)

## What and why

**The question.** A short-term planner or dispatch engineer has a fixed set of shovels and a truck fleet on a given
road network. They ask: how many trucks should each shovel get (fleet sizing), how should empty trucks be sent to
shovels in real time (dispatch), and what does each choice do to production (t/h), truck queues, the balance of
loading and hauling capacity (match factor) and cost per tonne?

**Why it matters.**

- Haulage is the largest single in-pit cost lever: a queueing study of truck–shovel systems states that haulage can
  account for as much as 60 % of the total operating cost of an open-pit mine [1].
- Haulage is also an exposure: powered haulage caused 13 of 33 US mining fatalities (39 %) and 426 of 4,792 non-fatal
  injuries (9 %) in 2025 [2]. Exposure grows with fleet size and traffic density, which links A1 to
  [B1](b1-traffic-proximity.md).
- Published RL dispatchers report gains (5.56 % productivity over an industry baseline [3]; about 10 % over plain PPO
  on the OpenMines simulator [4]), but each was measured on a different simulator against different baselines.
  A1 asks the question again, on one simulator, against strong classical baselines, with confidence intervals.

| Rung | Method | Role in A1 |
|---|---|---|
| Classical | [M1 Match factor + finite-source queue / MVA](../methods/m01-match-factor-queueing.md) | Closed-form fleet balance and shovel utilisation; the DES must reproduce it in the exponential case |
| Classical | [M2 Haulage DES](../methods/m02-haulage-des.md) with fixed allocation, nearest, SPTF and shortest-queue dispatchers | The engine and the classical baselines; `minehaulsim` reference, TS twin with exact-trace parity, SimPy oracle |
| Classical / industry SOTA | [M3 Two-stage LP dispatch](../methods/m03-lp-dispatch.md) | Upper-stage LP sets flow rates per shovel→dump path; a lower stage assigns trucks [8][9] |
| SOTA (learned) | [M4 PPO dispatch](../methods/m04-ppo-dispatch.md) in a GPU-vectorised env | PPO [10] trained on thousands of batched mine replicas, cross-checked in the DES |
| Beyond-SOTA (learned) | [M5 Attention fleet policy](../methods/m05-attention-fleet-policy.md) | Size-invariant across fleet sizes, unlike fixed-vector policies |

**Baseline comparison.** The learned policies (M4, M5) are compared with **SPTF** and **LP** on ≥ 30 paired seeds
(same mine, same breakdown and load-time draws per seed). "Beats SPTF / LP" is claimed only if the paired 95 %
confidence interval of the t/h difference excludes 0
([DEC-0016](../architecture/decisions/DEC-0016-pre-registered-decision-rule.md)).

## KPIs

| KPI | Unit | How it is computed |
|---|---|---|
| Production | t/h (per shovel and fleet) | Tonnes dumped at crusher and waste points in the DES event log ÷ simulated operating hours |
| Queue time | min per cycle (mean, P90) | Time between a truck's arrival at a shovel (or dump) and the start of its service, from the event log |
| Match factor | – | $MF = \dfrac{N_T\,t_L}{N_L\,T_c^{\ast}}$, with $N_T$, $N_L$ trucks and loaders, $t_L$ the time (min) to load one truck, $T_c^{\ast}$ the cycle time (min) without waiting [11]; $MF<1$ loaders wait, $MF>1$ trucks queue |
| Shovel utilisation | – | DES busy fraction; checked against $1-\pi_0$ of the finite-source M/M/1//N model [12] |
| Cost per tonne | USD/t | (truck-hours × truck rate + shovel-hours × shovel rate) ÷ tonnes; the hourly rates are documented inputs (UNVERIFIED — pinned at specification) |

## Assumptions and limits

- **Synthetic cycles.** Truck kinematics come from a generic parameter set, not an OEM chart; no real fleet log
  calibrates them. Results are statements about the model, not about any operating mine.
- **Analytical anchor.** With exponential load and travel times, the DES must match M/M/c and MVA closed forms; with
  realistic (non-exponential, physics-based) times it is the only valid estimator.
- **Transfer of RL claims.** The published gains [3][4] do not transfer to this simulator; only A1's own paired-seed
  results count.
- Validity range: one pit network, fleets of a few to a few tens of trucks per shift; no blending or plant-feed
  constraints beyond those in the LP.
- Simulation-grade twin, not a live digital twin: no telemetry feed.
